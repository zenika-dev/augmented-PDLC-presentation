The Augmented PDLC

A Systemic Design for AI‑Native Software Factories

The problem

A Whole‑System Redesign

AI agents can now write, test and ship code. How should IT delivery change around them?

People move back to engineering roles: they frame the intent, set the rules and make the calls. Agents take over the craft.

A tool rollout cannot carry that shift. It takes a whole delivery system: a lifecycle, a shared context, a platform, new roles and new measures.

Seven questions to address

    The wheel

    A Six-Phase Product Delivery Life Cycle

    Five phases form the loop: Discover, Design, Develop, Deploy, Deepen. Drop, the sixth, hangs outside it and retires what no longer earns its place.

    Each phase ends with a written output the next one builds on: a spec, tested code, a release record. Six gates pace the cycle: nothing moves on until its gate opens.

    The Idea

    One Idea's Journey in Twelve Shapes

    It starts as an idea. Design shapes it into a spec. Develop turns the spec into code, builds it and reviews it. Deploy ships it and keeps it running. Deepen observes it in use and analyses the results.

    What we learn becomes an opportunity, which is the next idea, and the wheel turns again. An idea that has served its time leaves through Drop.

    Quality gates

    A Gate on Every Passage

    Between two phases, the idea stops at a gate. Machines check first: tests, scans, schemas, the criteria the phase promised. Then a named person decides, and the platform records the decision.

    A gate opens on evidence, never on a claim. Each label shows what its gate checks.

    The Context Nexus

    Four Registers at the Core

    The memory that people and agents work from: four registers, one job each.

    Knowledge
    What we know: the domain, the architecture, conventions, past decisions and their reasons
    Intent
    What we want: specs, acceptance criteria, decisions to apply, the roadmap
    Contracts
    What we require: checkable rules with a threshold, from quality to security
    Operations
    What we do: runbooks, incident playbooks, deploy and rollback steps

    People write Intent and Contracts. Agents read them and report what they see; they never change them.

    Inheritance

    Org, BU, Team: Nested

    The Nexus follows the organisation's shape. The organisation sets what everyone inherits: security, conventions, shared tools. A business unit adds what its teams share, and a team adds its own domain.

    Extend
    Add rules the parent does not cover. Always allowed.
    Exception
    Depart from a parent rule, declared and approved by the parent's owner.
    Override
    Contradict a parent rule without saying so. Blocked.

    In human hands

    Intent Belongs to the Team

    Three registers come down from above. Intent does not: it exists only at team level, because what to build is always a team's call.

    The idea lives here. People write it; agents take it as their instructions. The sharper the intent, the better the output.

    The layers

    Five Domains Power the Wheel

    Under the wheel, thirteen capabilities sit in five domains, from the closest to the work down to the foundation.

    Discover
    Finds what is worth building, and what already exists
    Build
    Turns the spec into working code
    Contextualise
    Gives every agent the same context and the tools it may call
    Measure
    Proves the output is good, and keeps proving it
    Platform
    Routes, meters and secures every model call, on ground you control

    Thirteen layers

    Absorb the Volume, Then Open the Taps

    Agents multiply the specs, code and changes to review. Manual review alone gives way under that load.

    Deploy first the layers that absorb it: automated quality gates (L8), spec-driven development (L2), the Context Factory (L5). Then open the taps: more assistants, more agents.

    The flow

    Context Flows Out of the Nexus

    L5 Context Factory
    Collects, dates and sorts what the Nexus holds, then assembles each agent's brief for its task
    L4 Agent & MCP Registry
    Lists the agents and the tools each one may call. MCP is the standard way agents call tools.
    L6 Multi-agent Orchestration
    Hands the work to agents in order, each with its brief, and places the reviews

    What agents learn in test and in production flows back into the registers.

    Autonomous pipelines

    Nothing Starts Without the Key

    When an agent works under a person's account or a shared one, the trace names someone who did not make the decision. Nobody can audit that work, bill it or switch it off.

    So every agent gets its own identity: scoped, traced, revocable on its own. The key is checked at the AI Gateway (which model), the Registry (which tool) and the infrastructure (which data).

    Every artefact then has an author, and a named person who answers for it.

    Role lens

    People Own the Parts

    Eight roles run the engine. Each role lights the parts it owns.

    In each teamSpecification Engineer · Context Engineer · Orchestrator · Decision Guarantor

    For all teamsEvaluation Engineer · AgentOps · AI FinOps · Trace & Audit

    These are hats, not new hires. Left alone, the context, orchestration and decision hats land on the tech lead: about 0.7 FTE on top of the job (Zenika estimate).

    Measure

    Measure the System

    Delivery
    The four DORA metrics: lead time, deployment frequency, change failure rate, time to restore
    Quality
    Rework rate, gate pass rate, vulnerabilities introduced
    Transformation
    Share of workflows run with AI, reusable assets in use, teams on board
    Value
    Capacity moved to high-value work

    Freeze a baseline first; expect a dip before the gain.
